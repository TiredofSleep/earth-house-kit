# HEMP, BAMBOO, MYCELIUM — where living materials fit a fired-clay system
### v0.1 · research Sep 2026 (sources below) · the rules they were judged by: every part replaceable, nothing hidden that degrades, long life

| material and use | verdict | why | the test that proves it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hemp shiv briquettes as kiln fuel** | **ADOPT** (if supply exists) | ~18 MJ/kg dry, 2–4% ash, vs rice husk's 13.5–17.5 MJ/kg and 15–20% ash: hotter, cleaner, less grate-clogging; briquettes without binder | calorimetry of local shiv; fire a batch, log fuel per tonne, ash, any slagging on the ware |
| **hemp as a cover crop on worked-out pit benches** | **TEST** | up to ~13 t/ha dry matter reported on ex-mine soil; builds soil. But a Malvern clay pit is probably not contaminated, and hemp dislikes compacted, wet, acid clay without drainage and compost | two seasons of plots against native grass/legume mixes: yield, soil carbon, infiltration, bulk density. Arkansas license ($50 application). Fibre hemp stays legal under the Nov 2026 federal definition |
| **hemp fibre in lime / cocciopesto render (1–3%)** | **TEST** | 2–4% fibre raised render flexural strength 40–70%. Fibre embrittles in alkali over time, which is acceptable in a renewable layer | render panels on fired units: cracking at 28/90 days, then 1–2 years outdoors, pull-off and flexure on the aged coat |
| **hemp or straw as a pore-former in the unit core** | **TEST** | straw-type additives at 5% cut body λ 16–23%; hemp burns out cleaner than husk (no silica, no cristobalite risk) | fire bars at 0/3/5/8% beside rice husk: strength, absorption, λ, freeze-thaw, black-coring |
| hemp hurd as loose cell fill | **AVOID as primary** | rice husk insulates better (λ 0.040–0.046 vs 0.055–0.065), its silica skin deters pests, it settles less, and it's near-free in Arkansas | blend test only if hurd is surplus |
| hempcrete walls | **AVOID in the shell** | cast-in, non-removable, and a second wall system; IRC Appendix BL allows it only as non-structural infill | optional for interior partitions |
| **treated whole bamboo as roof framing, verandahs, shade structures, under cover, visible, replaceable** | **TEST, then ADOPT** | untreated 2–5 years outdoors; borate (or borate + oil heat treatment), kept dry and ventilated: ~15–25 years per member with planned replacement; tensile strength 115–309 MPa | ISO 22157 tests of local culms, full-scale clamped joints (ISO 22156), borate assay, yearly inspection of a sacrificial member |
| **bamboo scaffolding and formwork** (e.g. the central mast for the ribbed dome) | **ADOPT** | temporary, reusable, cheap | — |
| bamboo instead of steel rods, tendons or hoop bands | **AVOID** | ~1/13 the stiffness of steel, creeps (composites failed at 65% of ultimate within 100 h), shrinks and swells with moisture: it can't hold prestress; joints reach ~30–50% of the culm | — |
| "petrified" / mineralized / ceramized whole bamboo | **AVOID (for now)** | CaCO₃ and silica mineralization are lab-scale on small wood samples; silicate leached ~50% in a year; biomorphic SiC needs >1,400 °C and becomes a brittle ceramic; densified bamboo (~1 GPa) destroys the culm | watch the research; nothing is buildable yet |
| bamboo or hemp as hidden ties in masonry | **AVOID** | embedded, wet, alkaline: the worst service for plant fibre; no long-term data | — |
| mycelium or bacteria as self-healing agents in the structure | **AVOID** | dry-stacked fired clay has no mortar joints to heal, and fired ceramic can't heal biologically; it would put organisms and nutrients into walls meant to stay dry. Fungal healing (Trichoderma reesei) is a lab proof of concept; best healed cracks ~1.3 mm | — |
| bacterial additive in the cocciopesto render | optional **TEST**, low priority | bacteria helped cement-lime masonry recover up to 33% of bond strength (TU Delft); plain lime already heals fine cracks, and the render is renewed on schedule anyway | render panels with and without, cracked and wetted |
| mycelium panels, interior acoustic | optional **TEST** | light, λ ~0.03–0.05, but 0.17–1.1 MPa, absorbs 40–580% water, combustible; fine only dry, visible, replaceable | E84 fire, mould at 80–90% RH, water uptake |

**In short:** hemp joins the kit as a better kiln fuel, a render fibre and a pit crop worth trialling.
Bamboo joins as the roof framing and verandahs of a building whose shell is fired clay, visible and on a
replacement schedule, never as its steel. Mycelium stays out of the structure. The clay shell doesn't
need healing, and the lime already heals itself.
